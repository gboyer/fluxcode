Why byte-shuffled zstd loses to raw zstd on calibrated-ADC & decimal float64s

Across several datasets, bshuf zstd is 2–3× larger than raw zstd—for example, 52.48 vs 18.33 bps on CWRU Vibration, 29.63 vs 9.48 bps on MIT-BIH ECG, and 16.63 vs 6.93 bps on UCI Power. Calibrated-ADC and decimal-rounded series tend to have relatively few distinct values (CWRU normal_48k_DE has only 2,254 unique values across 485,643 samples), so raw zstd's LZ77 matcher can copy entire repeated 8-byte floats in a single match. Byte-shuffling splits those 8-byte values across separate streams, and for non-power-of-two steps like 0.005 or 2.086e-4, the six low mantissa bytes look nearly random on their own and no longer match together. fluxcode improves on both baselines by 2–7× because it recovers the underlying integer grid first, then applies per-block delta coding (orders 0–3) and bit-plane zstd.

Hidden rational/ADC grids & float32 recovery

Comparing fluxcode nf=0 (Params(noise_floor_sigma=0)) against fluxcode bin nf=0 (grids='binary') isolates the savings from decimal, rational, and scaled-ADC grid detection on real data.

  • CWRU Vibration (ADC calibration step): Raw accelerometer waveforms sit on a ~2.086e-4 calibration step. Scaled-grid detection saves +24.5% across the group (up to +32.1% on normal_48k_DE) at ~1 ULP (2.8e-17) error when noise_floor_sigma=0.
  • MIT-BIH ECG (0.005 mV ADC step): 11-bit WFDB-212 counts scaled by 200 counts/mV land on an exact scaled decimal grid, compressing to 4.14 bps bit-exact across all 2.6M samples, which is +56.7% smaller than binary-only grids (9.57 bps).
  • UCI Appliances (averaged 1/3 & 1/4 rational grids): 10-minute averaged weather and indoor sensors (T_out, Press_mm_hg, Windspeed) sit on exact rational multiples of 1/3 and 1/4, compressing to 0.92–1.31 bps at 0.0000% error (+54.6% to +66.7% smaller than binary grids).
  • UCI Hydraulic (multi-rate industrial PLC): All 5 channels (100 Hz pressure/motor power, 10 Hz flow, 1 Hz temperature/vibration; 3.99M samples) decode bit-exact at nf=0 with a +33.4% group-wide grid gain (up to +54.7% on VS1_1Hz).
  • ERA5 Weather (float32-to-decimal recovery): temp_2m_f32 and pressure_f32 are cast through float32 and promoted back to float64, introducing ~10−7 binary rounding noise. fluxcode recovers the exact decimal grid at the same bit rates as the clean float64 originals (4.95 bps and 4.10 bps, +52.4% and +57.9% smaller than binary grids).

Real-world signal findings

  • Band-limited sigma-delta ADCs (IRIS 24-bit seismic): The 40 Hz broadband seismometer (IU.ANMO.00 during the 2023-02-06 M7.8 Kahramanmaras earthquake) uses a 24-bit sigma-delta ADC with a steep hardware anti-alias filter. Because the signal is smooth and band-limited, higher-order delta predictors work well: raw counts compress to 4.16–4.37 bps (0.002% max error), and calibrated ground velocity (ANMO_BHZ_mps, ~10−4 m/s scale) compresses to 4.96 bps (0.023% max error), which is 7× smaller than raw zstd (34.46 bps) and 9× smaller than bshuf zstd (44.95 bps).
  • Effort tradeoffs (BIDMC clinical): On most datasets, effort=1 (fastest) lands within 1–2% of the default effort=4. On 125 Hz clinical waveforms (BIDMC PPG, respiration, and ECG), effort=4 reaches 4.84 bps versus 5.57 bps at effort=1 (a 13% reduction from better predictor and codec selection), while effort=5 improves that by only 0.8% (4.80 bps).
  • Decimal sentinels vs NaN (NOAA Buoy): Legacy buoy feeds encode missing values as in-band sentinels (99.0, 999.0, 9999.0), which inflate the block range and create large delta spikes. Replacing those sentinels with IEEE NaN moves missingness into fluxcode's non-finite mask: WTMP drops from 1.92 bps (WTMP_raw) to 1.28 bps (WTMP_nan, −33%), and WVHT (66.7% missing) drops from 4.08 bps to 2.79 bps (−32%), while remaining bit-exact on every finite sample.

Encode & decode throughput

Single-threaded throughput (NUMBA_NUM_THREADS=1, default Params()) is measured in MB/s of uncompressed float64 input (8 bytes/sample). On full-length series, decode runs at 2.5–5.0 GB/s and encode at 400–1,000 MB/s. Smartphone IMU is slower (140 MB/s encode, 480 MB/s decode) because each trial contains only 761–1,008 samples, so per-call Python and zstd setup overhead dominates.

  • Regular and gapped clocks add minimal overhead: UCI Power (2.08M rows, 1-min), UCI AirQuality (1-h), UCI Appliances (10-min), ERA5 (1-h), and NOAA Buoy (10-min with gaps) add only +0.006 to +0.028 bps. SKAB's 1 Hz industrial PLC clock has occasional drops and drift, adding +0.863 bps.
  • Jittered smartphone IMU clocks benefit from time_error: Real ~50 Hz phone clocks (dt 16–177 ms) cost +7.92 to +8.98 bps when stored exactly. Setting Params(time_error=0.15) smooths out the hardware jitter and saves ~1.65–1.68 bps while preserving sample order and keeping every timestamp within tolerance.
  • Irregular event clocks are left exact (Binance trades): Without timestamps, Binance trades average 4.14 bps across price and quantity, with BTCUSDT_price at 3.03 bps bit-exact on the $0.01 grid. Adding 1.11M exact irregular millisecond arrival timestamps adds +5.973 bps (9.00 bps total for exact cent prices and millisecond timestamps). Because trade arrivals have no periodic cadence, time_error=0.05 and 0.15 leave them unmodified instead of distorting event timing.
